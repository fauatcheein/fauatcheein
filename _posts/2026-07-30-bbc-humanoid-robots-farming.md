title: "BBC News: humanoid robots the 'next stage' in farming"
---
BBC News featured our research at Harper Adams University on humanoid robots for agriculture, as part of its Farmwatch series.

Most farm robots today are built for a single job; as Fernando told the BBC, each one "is basically an adhoc solution". A humanoid robot shares "similar capabilities" with people, so it can be programmed for many tasks, such as harvesting, planting and pruning, and could work faster once those tasks are automated. Farming practices were designed around the human body, which is why a humanoid can take on work that people do today.

The aim is collaboration, not replacement. Robots and farm workers will share the same space, so the robots also need social skills. In his words, "the robot and ourselves in the same environment should co-exist in some sense."
